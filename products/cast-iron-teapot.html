About it

Cast iron is for the hour where you pour a second and a third cup. The wall is 4mm of iron with a thermal mass to match, and a full pot under its lid is still properly hot around forty minutes after brewing — closer to fifteen for glass and twenty-five for stoneware.

The inside is enamel-lined. That matters: bare cast iron used for tea will rust and will pass iron taste into a delicate leaf, whereas enamel gives you a neutral, non-reactive surface you can simply rinse. It also means this is a teapot and not a kettle — it must never go on a hob or over a flame, which would craze the enamel.

Capacity is 24 fluid ounces. There is a removable stainless mesh basket, a hinged arched handle that folds back flat, and a raised hobnail pattern cast into the body. It weighs 2.9 pounds empty and about 4.4 pounds full, which is a real consideration if pouring one-handed is difficult — a lighter glass or stoneware pot may simply suit you better, and we would rather say so.

Care
  • Rinse with hot water and dry immediately after every use. Do not soak.
  • Hand wash only. Never put cast iron in a dishwasher.
  • Never place on a stovetop, hob, hotplate or open flame. It is a teapot, not a kettle.
  • Do not use abrasive pads or scouring powder on the enamel lining.
  • Dry the exterior thoroughly; chips in the powder coat will rust if left wet.
